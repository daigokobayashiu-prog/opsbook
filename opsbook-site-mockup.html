The Problem

Your organization already knows how to be ready. That knowledge just isn't connected.

Every organization holds enormous operational knowledge: plans, procedures, playbooks, policies, exercise records, incident reports, after-action reports, and the experience carried by your people. Because it lives in separate systems and separate heads, nobody can see the full picture.

Gaps Stay Invisible

Nobody owns the full picture of what's actually covered and what isn't.

Preparedness Decays

Plans go stale the moment the organization changes underneath them.

Lessons Get Lost

Findings from an exercise or a real incident rarely make it back into the next plan.

Response Depends on Memory

Under pressure, teams fall back on what one or two people remember, not on what the organization has actually learned.

The Foundation

Everything your organization creates should make it more prepared.

Opsbook connects the sources of operational knowledge that normally sit apart. Instead of existing as isolated files and one-off events, they continuously inform and strengthen each other inside a single, shared intelligence layer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about the two paths.

What is the difference between the two paths?

Door A is a facilitated tabletop exercise delivered for you on Opsbook, with an optional program build. Door B puts Opsbook into a program you already run, with expert help, so your own facilitators run the exercises. Both paths write to the same record.

Can I buy a single facilitated exercise?

Yes. A single exercise is a complete engagement. It runs on Opsbook, so you keep the after-action report, the findings, and the record of every decision, and you can extend into a program or the full platform later.

Do I need Opsbook facilitators to use the software?

No. If you have facilitators in house, Door B gives them role-based workspaces, live injects, and automated after-action reports. Opsbook specialists help with the deployment, and your team runs exercises on its own schedule.

What happens after my first exercise?

You receive an after-action report with strengths, gaps, and recommended actions. From there you can run another facilitated exercise, start a first-90-days program build, or move the program onto the platform for your own team to run.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about the platform.

What is Opsbook?

Opsbook is an AI-powered operational readiness platform. It connects an organization's documents, playbooks, exercises, and after-action reports into one continuously improving intelligence layer, so preparedness gaps get identified, fixed, and verified over time instead of resetting after every exercise.

How is Opsbook different from tabletop exercise software?

Tabletop exercise software runs isolated events. Opsbook treats exercises as one input into a continuous readiness cycle: every exercise, evaluation, and after-action report feeds a shared organizational intelligence layer that carries findings forward into the next cycle.

Does Opsbook's AI make decisions during an incident?

No. Opsbook's AI helps understand your organization, discover gaps, draft playbooks, and analyze outcomes, but every recommendation routes through human review and approval. Opsbook does not make containment or notification decisions on your behalf.

How does Opsbook work before, during, and after an incident?

Before an incident, Opsbook identifies what should be tested and improved. During an incident, it assembles the right people, actions, decisions, and evidence into a coordinated response plan. Afterward, it learns from what happened and strengthens the next response.

What does Opsbook replace?

Most resilience programs run on static PDFs, outdated playbooks, tribal knowledge, and ad hoc coordination in Slack or Teams. Opsbook unifies all of it into one governed intelligence layer, so preparedness, response, and evidence live in a single connected system.
